# 🎬 Netflix Data Analysis Project

**Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of Netflix’s global catalog using Python**, uncovering genre trends, regional preferences, and actionable insights for content strategy.

---

## 🔎 Overview

This project analyzes a Netflix dataset (`netflix.csv`) to explore patterns in content production, distribution, and viewer preferences.  

The notebook ( covers:  
- Data cleaning & wrangling  
- Visualization & insight generation  
- Strategic thinking from a data-driven business perspective  

Key questions addressed:  
- What do content trends reveal about Netflix’s growth?  
- How can Netflix optimize its catalog for global and regional success?

---

## 📂 Dataset

- **8,800+ titles** (Movies + TV Shows)  
- **Fields:** Title, Director, Cast, Country, Date Added, Release Year, Rating, Duration, Genre, Type (Movie/TV Show)

---

## 🛠️ Tools & Libraries

- **Environment:** Python (Google Colab)  
- **Libraries:** pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn

---

## 📈 Key Insights

### 1. Content Growth
- Content surged post-2015, peaking in 2020 (pandemic-driven).  
- Bulk uploads often occur in Jan, Jul, Oct, Nov.  
➡️ Netflix pursued a bulk-acquisition strategy to expand rapidly.

### 2. Movies vs TV Shows
- Movies = ~70% of catalog (avg duration ~106 min)  
- TV Shows = ~30% (avg 1.2–1.3 seasons, dominated by limited series)  
➡️ Movies drive scale; TV shows drive retention — an opportunity gap vs competitors.

### 3. Regional Trends
- 🇺🇸 & 🇮🇳 lead in movie production  
- 🇯🇵 & 🇰🇷 lead in TV shows (anime & K-dramas)  
➡️ Growth levers vary by region — “one-size-fits-all” doesn’t work.

### 4. Genre Patterns
- Dominant: Drama, International, Comedy  
- Mid-tier: Action, Family, Independent  
- Underrepresented: Classics, Sci-Fi, Kids  
➡️ Catalog skews mature; family & kids content is under-served.

### 5. Ratings
- TV-MA (Mature): ~40%  
- TV-14: ~20%  
➡️ Netflix’s sweet spot = young adults + mature audiences.

---

## 🚀 Business Recommendations

### 📍 Global Strategy
- Expand movies in US & India; diversify into Sci-Fi, Thrillers, Family  
- Double down on anime & K-dramas globally  
- Strengthen family/kids content to capture households

### 📍 Regional Growth
- **India:** Regional originals (Tamil/Telugu/Hindi) + mobile-first pricing  
- **Korea:** Keep dramas, grow thrillers/sci-fi  
- **Japan:** Position as “Home of Anime” + boost family-friendly anime  
- **UK:** Prestige dramas + docuseries; leverage BBC/ITV partnerships

### 📍 Content & Genre Strategy
- Add 200–300 K-dramas yearly  
- Increase kids/family content from ~10%  
- Diversify into underrepresented genres (Sci-Fi, Classics, Fantasy)

### 📍 Platform Innovation
- Interactive formats (e.g., Bandersnatch-style shows)  
- AI-powered localization for faster dubs/subtitles  
- Short-form episodes for mobile-first markets

### 📍 Competitive Positioning
- **Disney+:** Counter kids/family dominance with anime & fantasy  
- **Prime Video:** Bundle lifestyle content (fitness, food, music)  
- **Local OTTs:** Secure exclusive deals with regional stars/studios

## 🏁 Conclusion

Netflix’s catalog shows **clear strengths and gaps**:

✅ Strong movie library, diverse genres, rising international content  
❌ Weak in family/kids content, limited long-running TV shows, heavy mature skew  

**Strategic Playbook:**  
- **India:** Regional originals + mobile-friendly pricing  
- **Japan:** Anime + kids  
- **Korea:** Expand into thrillers  
- **UK:** Docuseries + prestige dramas  
- **Global:** Interactive + AI-powered localization  

➡️ **Goal:** Netflix can become *“globally available, locally unbeatable.”*
